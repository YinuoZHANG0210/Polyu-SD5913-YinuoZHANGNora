# Who Is the Author?

在我之前的一部 AI 影像项目《[Antipode](https://youtu.be/cndMIjVstkw)》（Zhang, 2026）中，我尝试让作品的形式本身成为它所讨论的问题。这部影片完全由人工智能生成，却讨论 AI 伦理、创造与作者性，形成了一个循环。影片沿着两条叙事展开：在光下，一位导演站在舞台中央，以冷静而笃定的语言审判 AI 对艺术的污染与亵渎，并点燃一场数字猎巫；在暗处，镜面、裂痕与低语相互缠绕，影像在自身内部不断崩解与重组。当两条叙事逐渐靠近，观众发现那位发起审判的导演本身也是 AI，或者说，是 AI 根据人类的语言、心理与恐惧生成的一场表演。

In one of my previous AI film projects, *[Antipode](https://youtu.be/cndMIjVstkw)* (Zhang, 2026), I tried to make the form of the work become part of the question it asks. The film was generated entirely with artificial intelligence, yet it concerns AI ethics, creation, and authorship. It unfolds through two narratives. In the light, a director stands on a stage and calmly condemns AI for contaminating and desecrating art, starting a kind of digital witch hunt. In the dark, mirrors, cracks, and whispers intertwine while images collapse and reform within themselves. As the two narratives move closer together, the audience discovers that the director who began the judgement is also AI—or perhaps a performance generated by AI from human language, psychology, and fear.

这个项目使我开始认真思考：如果我指挥一个系统生成作品，这是谁的作品？我的答案并不是“AI 只是工具”，或者“AI 才是作者”。我更愿意说，这部影片是我与 AI 的共同创作，只是这种共同创作并不平等，也并不意味着我能把责任完全交给AI。

This project made me ask seriously: if I direct a system that produces the work, whose work is it? My answer is neither that AI is “only a tool” nor that AI is the sole author. I see the film as a collaboration between AI and myself. However, this collaboration is not equal, and it does not allow me to transfer responsibility to the system.

影片的框架并不是由我首先独立完成、再让 AI 执行的剧本。而是从最开始的概念设定，到叙事的推进方式、人物的语言和画面的气氛，都是在我与 AI 经过多轮对话、生成、筛选和推翻中逐渐形成的。AI 曾提出一些我原本没有想到的视觉联想与叙事方向，有些意外生成的结果也迫使我重新理解自己的想法。因此，它不只是代替三维软件、实景拍摄或后期软件完成技术工作。它会回应我的输入，也会通过它能够生成和不能生成的内容，影响我所认为“可能出现”的作品呈现。

The framework of the film was not a finished script that I wrote alone and then asked AI to execute. From the initial concept to the structure of the narrative, the director’s language, and the atmosphere of the images, the work developed through repeated conversations, generations, selections, and revisions between AI and me. AI suggested visual associations and narrative directions that I had not previously imagined. Some unexpected outputs also forced me to reconsider my original ideas. In this sense, AI did more than replace the technical work that might otherwise be done through 3D software, a camera, or post-production tools. It responded to my inputs, but it also shaped my sense of what kinds of work were possible through what it could and could not generate.

但 AI 不能替我决定作品的最终意义。举例来说，它可以生成看起来有力量的画面，却不能判断那个画面是否真的服务于主题本身；它可以模仿批判 AI 的语调，却不能判断这种批判是否只是另一种漂亮而空洞的姿态。影片最终批判什么，哪些结果被保留，观众应在何处感到不安，仍然取决于我出于我的经验与知识积累作出的选择。更重要的是，作为创作者，我必须对这些选择承担责任。AI 没有个人经验，也不会承担作品被误读、伤害他人或重复偏见的后果。

However, AI could not decide what the film meant. It could generate an image that looked powerful, but it could not judge whether that image actually served the theme. It could imitate the language of AI criticism, but it could not tell whether that criticism was meaningful or simply another beautiful and empty gesture. What the film criticises, which outputs I keep, and where I want the audience to feel uncomfortable are still decisions made by me. More importantly, I am responsible for those decisions. AI has no personal experience, and it does not bear the consequences if a work is misunderstood, harms someone, or repeats an existing bias.

不过，这种责任并不代表我完全掌握了作品方向的全部控制权。在影片中，审判 AI 的导演最终成为被审判的对象；在创作中，我也意识到自己同样处于这样的循环中。我以为自己在使用 AI，但它的语言模式、图像习惯和训练数据中的文化偏见，也在影响我的判断。它可能把某一种视觉风格呈现为“自然”的选择，把某一种情绪呈现为“电影感”，而我必须学习辨认这些倾向，而不是无意识地全盘接受它们。

At the same time, responsibility does not mean that I fully controlled the work. In the film, the director who judges AI eventually becomes the object of judgement. During the project, I realised that I was also inside a similar loop. I thought I was using AI, but its language patterns, visual habits, and cultural biases from its training data were also shaping my judgement. It might present one visual style as the natural choice, or make one kind of emotion seem more “cinematic” than another. My task was to recognise these tendencies rather than accept them unconsciously.

我目前只部分理解 AI 的运作方式。我知道如何使用提示词、参考图、反复生成与筛选，让最终的生成结果结果更接近我的意图；但我并不真正理解模型的底层代码、训练数据和计算结构。这种有限的理解让我能够创作，却也让我经常只能从结果倒推过程，而不能完全预见系统会如何回应。

At present, I only partly understand how AI systems work. I know how to use prompts, reference images, repeated generation, and selection to move results closer to my intention. But I do not fully understand the underlying code, training data, or computational structure of the model. This partial understanding allows me to create, but it also means that I often have to infer the process from the output rather than predict exactly how the system will respond.

如果我更深入地理解 AI 的运作机制，我认为关于作者性的答案不会彻底改变。AI 仍会是一个重要的共创者，因为它带来的意外和限制确实参与了作品的形成。但我的贡献会变得更主动、更精确。我将更能预测系统的偏向，设计生成的条件，并区分哪些结果来自我的决定，哪些只是模型惯性的产物。理解技术不会让我重新获得绝对控制权，它会让我更清楚地知道，我的控制在哪里结束，而系统的影响又从哪里开始。

If I understood AI more deeply, I do not think the answer about authorship would completely change. AI would still be an important collaborator because its surprises and limitations genuinely shape the work. But my contribution would become more active and precise. I would be better able to anticipate the system’s tendencies, design the conditions of generation, and distinguish between results that came from my decisions and results that merely came from the model’s habits. Understanding the technology would not give me absolute control again. Instead, it would help me see more clearly where my control ends and where the system’s influence begins.
