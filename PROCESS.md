# Process

## Tools and extent of AI use

I used Codex for brainstorming, structuring, drafting, translation, editing, Markdown formatting, reference formatting, and help with the Git workflow. Its contribution went beyond proofreading: substantial parts of the essay’s wording and organisation came from AI-generated drafts based on my description of my project and my position on authorship.

I began by describing my previous AI film, *Antipode*, in Chinese. I explained its two narratives and the revelation that the director condemning AI is himself generated by AI. I also supplied my central position: I regard the film as a collaboration between AI and myself, while my intentions and judgements guide its direction. My initial view was that deeper technical understanding would increase my contribution without making me the sole author.

This account concerns AI assistance with the essay. It is not a complete record of the tools used to produce the earlier film.

## How the essay developed

I first asked Codex to discuss and develop my ideas in the conversation before writing them into a file. It produced a Chinese draft, then a bilingual version. At my request, it placed each Chinese paragraph above its English translation in `README.md` so that I could compare and revise them.

I edited the Chinese and repeatedly asked Codex to update the corresponding English. My changes included making the film’s circular structure explicit, specifying live-action filming as part of the workflow AI could replace, and explaining that my choices draw on my experience and accumulated knowledge. I also revised the wording about responsibility and control. The current README contains the English essay.

I supplied the film’s YouTube link and asked for an APA reference. Codex retrieved the public video title, uploader, and publication date, then added the reference and linked in-text mentions. This was a metadata check, not a verification of the film’s content. I later asked it to check the word count and improve the Markdown hierarchy; it added four section headings and bold emphasis to selected claims.

Codex also checked the repository structure, helped resolve a Git commit that was waiting for a message, and completed a local commit when I asked it to. These were workflow tasks rather than contributions to the argument.

## What I kept

I kept the distinction the AI draft developed between shared authorship and responsibility for the work. My original explanation emphasised collaboration and my control over the direction of the film. The draft helped articulate why acknowledging AI’s contribution does not mean handing over responsibility for my choices. This gave me a clearer way to explain how I can consider AI a collaborator while still answering for what I choose to present.

I also kept the bilingual drafting format during revision because it let me adjust the argument in Chinese before asking for changes to the English.

## What I revised rather than accepting unchanged

I did not reject an entire AI-written draft. My corrections were more specific. One draft described the film’s direction and selection of outputs simply as “decisions made by me”. I revised the Chinese to explain that those choices come from my experience and accumulated knowledge, then asked Codex to reflect this in English. The original wording identified who made the decisions but left out the basis of my judgement, which matters to my argument about authorship.

I also changed the statement that AI could not decide “what the film meant” to say that it could not decide the work’s ultimate meaning “for me”. This makes my position more precise: AI influences how the work develops, but I still have to judge whether its outputs express what I want to say.

These revisions showed me that plausible wording can still leave out the part of an argument that matters most to me.
