# Process

## My starting point

I chose craft and authorship as the direction for this essay and brought my previous film, *Antipode*, as its central example. Before asking for a draft, I described the film in Chinese, including its two narratives and the revelation that the director condemning AI is himself AI-generated. I also stated my position: the film is a collaboration between AI and myself, while my intentions and judgements guide its direction. My initial view was that understanding the system more deeply would increase my contribution without making me the sole author.

## How I used Codex

I used Codex for discussion, structure, drafting, Chinese–English translation, editing, Markdown formatting, citation checks, and Git assistance. Its help included generating prose and developing the organisation of the essay, not only correcting grammar. The project account and initial position came from me; Codex helped turn them into drafts that I revised through the conversation and in Chinese. In the final review, I authorised further corrections, and Codex edited awkward English and made the connection to learning programming more explicit.

I first asked for brainstorming in the conversation rather than immediate file changes. I then requested a Chinese draft and a bilingual version with corresponding paragraphs. After editing the Chinese, I repeatedly asked Codex to update the English. The current README contains the English essay. I also supplied the film's YouTube link and selected the connections I wanted to explore with Beattie, Lovelace, and Ihde's account of mediation.

This note records assistance with the essay, not the full production process of the earlier film.

## What I kept, and why

I kept the distinction developed in the AI-assisted draft between sharing authorship and taking responsibility for my choices. My starting point emphasised collaboration and my control over the direction of the film. This distinction helped me explain why acknowledging AI's contribution does not remove my responsibility for the outputs I select and present.

I also kept the bilingual format during revision because it allowed me to work on the argument in Chinese before updating the English.

## What I rejected or revised, and why

I rejected the certainty of the draft's phrase “I would be better able to” when discussing the effects of deeper technical understanding. During the citation review, Codex flagged that this was an expectation rather than a result established by the cited sources. I explicitly asked it to replace the phrase with “I would hope to become better able to”. I kept the aspiration but rejected presenting the improvement as assured.

I also revised wording that described the film's direction simply as my decisions. I wanted the essay to explain the basis of those decisions: my experience and accumulated knowledge. Naming the decision-maker alone did not fully express my position on authorship. These were specific corrections, not a claim that I discarded an entire draft.
